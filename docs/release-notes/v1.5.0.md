# animaEngine 1.5.0

The characters come to life, and the overlay finds a place in your day.
Most of it is off until you turn it on.

## What you'll notice

- **They are alive, if you want them to be.** Tap a character, even
  outside edit mode, and it hops, says one of its own lines and plays
  its sound, with a few hearts. Characters can follow one another, play
  tag, greet each other when they meet and bump into each other; they
  fade in and out, keep hours of their own, and can be livelier in the
  morning and slower at night.
- **Around your day.** Reminders every so often or at a set time, said
  by a character in a bubble — and, if you like, as a desktop
  notification with a sound. A focus timer. Bedtime. Snooze for a
  while from the tray. While you are away they doze, and greet you when
  you come back. They step aside while a game, a video or a
  presentation fills the screen, or while an app you choose is in
  front, and can live behind your windows instead of over them.
- **Editing.** Undo and redo (`Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z`). Select several
  characters — Shift+click or a rectangle — and move, change, group,
  line up or space them together; drags snap. Copy, cut and paste
  characters, or paste a picture copied in another app. Throw one.
- **Scenes and files.** Save what is on screen under a name, with a
  picture, and switch between scenes from the Scene tab, the palette,
  the tray or on a schedule. Share a scene or one character as a single
  file, save a character as a GIF, and back up everything in one file to
  restore on another machine.
- **Sounds at the volume you want**: one volume for everything and one
  for each character, quiet at bedtime, mute from the tray, a shortcut
  or D-Bus.
- **Without a mouse.** `Shift+F10` or the Menu key opens a character's
  right-click menu, `Ctrl+A` selects every character, `N` steps through
  them, and `Tab` moves through the settings panel. The F-keys can be
  bound.
- **Lighter.** The overlay barely works while nothing moves: hidden,
  snoozed, paused or asleep it wakes a few times a second, where native
  Wayland used to draw sixty frames a second, and a hidden overlay on X11
  as many as a shown one. With several monitors, each one's overlay
  draws only when something on it changes. It also starts sooner and
  sets up the GPU with less memory and fewer threads.
- **In your language, more of it.** The tray, the presets, the reasons a
  file is refused, a first run's characters and what delivers the global
  shortcuts were English in every language; they are translated now, in
  all ten. A count is said as your language says that number — "60 de
  minute", "5 postaci".

## Fixed

A selection of what was wrong before 1.5:

- Logging out or shutting down with the app running lost whatever it
  had not saved yet — edits still in edit mode, a pause from the tray.
  It saves on the way out now, as its own Quit always did.
- Installed from a package and started from the desktop's menu, the app
  made an `assets` folder in your home directory, for its demo
  characters' pictures. They live in its data directory now; an
  `~/assets/demo` folder left behind can be deleted.
- Saving never wrote the sprite groups back: a deleted character's id
  stayed in its group, and a character added later under the same id
  joined that group at the next start.
- Native Wayland drew sixty frames a second whatever the scene did, and
  edit mode did on both backends.
- Sounds stopped for good when the sound server restarted.
- Leaving edit mode or hiding the overlay in the middle of a drag left
  the character frozen, or carried it to the pointer afterwards; on
  native Wayland with several monitors, hiding left the other monitors'
  characters on screen.
- The right-click menu of a character on another monitor opened on the
  wrong one; opened from the keyboard, no key reached it there and a
  screen reader did not see it — it now opens on the panel's monitor.
- On native Wayland at a scale above 1 — 150 %, 200 % — the settings
  panel came out twice its size, clicks on it missed, and everything
  was blurred. Each monitor is now drawn sharp at its own scale.
- The settings panel's footer covered the last row of a long tab, and
  the panel's text fields had no copy and paste.
- The settings panel grew wider in some languages, on the Shortcuts tab
  in all of them, and a screen reader found some controls without a
  name.
- On native Wayland, starting the app again while its characters were
  hidden did nothing; it shows them, as on X11.
- `H` and `I` only wrote to the log: `H` opens the Keybindings tab now,
  every shortcut, and `I` the Inspector. `+` raises the opacity on a US
  keyboard too, and on native Wayland `Ctrl+Shift+H` hides the overlay
  in edit mode, as on X11.
- After an upgrade, a new action's default could take a shortcut you had
  set, and the Keybindings tab could not remove a new action's default.

## Changed

- **On a laptop with an NVIDIA GPU beside an integrated one**, the
  NVIDIA GPU is left asleep: loading its driver woke it, and starting
  and quitting each took some 3 s longer. `prime-run` still draws on it.
- **On X11 with a screen scale factor above 1**, the characters are
  drawn at it too, as the panel always was: at 200 % they were half the
  size they are on native Wayland.
- **Messages show outside edit mode too**, in the bottom-right corner,
  and clicks go through them: one raised while the panel was closed — a
  reload, a refused file, a pause from the tray — was gone before anyone
  opened it.
- **Cycle character is on `N`.** `Tab` belongs to the settings panel's
  focus, where it never cycled on X11 and only once on native Wayland.
  A config that still had `Tab` there as it came moves to `N`; one set
  by hand stays.
- **A config written by a newer version no longer costs the whole
  file**, nor does a scene, a shared file or a backup: what this version
  cannot read is left out, with a banner or a toast, and kept in the
  file for the version that wrote it.
- **Five things are on from the start**, each with its switch: stepping
  aside for full-screen apps, dozing after ten minutes away and the
  welcome back, the little effects, and snapping while dragging.

## Compatibility

- **Upgrading:** additive. A 1.4 config loads with every value kept —
  configs written by 1.0 and 1.4 themselves are test fixtures now.
- **Going back to 1.4 or earlier:** they read a 1.5 config as long as it
  holds nothing they lack. Once it does — a character that follows
  another or plays tag, say — they start with the default scene and keep
  your file beside it as `config.toml.bak-corrupt`; copy it back after
  upgrading again. (Checked both ways with a build of 1.4.0.)
- **D-Bus:** new methods `StartFocus`, `StopFocus`, `Snooze`, `Wake`,
  `NextScene`, `SetMuted` and `ToggleMute`; the old ones are unchanged.
- **Flatpak:** two more read-only D-Bus names, for the idle time
  (`org.gnome.Mutter.IdleMonitor`) and the battery
  (`org.freedesktop.UPower`).
- No change to the CLI, or to where the config and the data live; 1.5
  adds folders inside them: `scenes/` beside `config.toml`, and `demo/`
  in the data directory for the first characters' pictures. See
  [stability-policy.md](../stability-policy.md).

## Known gaps in verification

Everything in 1.5 was run in the headless test rig on both backends —
sway with software rendering, a private D-Bus and PipeWire, stand-ins
for the portals, Mutter's idle monitor and the notification server —
plus the X11 shortcuts and two monitors on a real X server, the `.deb`
and the AppImage built by the release scripts and run, and the unit
tests under Wine.
Still to be checked on a real GNOME session, with a GPU, a sound card,
Orca and the AppIndicator tray: the list in
[gnome-checklist-1.5.md](../gnome-checklist-1.5.md). The Windows build
compiles and its unit tests pass under Wine; it has not run on real
Windows. The new text is machine-translated in nine of the ten
languages.

The full list is in [CHANGELOG.md](../../CHANGELOG.md).
